01 / Decision brief

Act on what the evidence supports.

The loan laptop is missing. A supplied inventory lists an offline file with addresses and confidential support needs. Access to that file is unconfirmed, and the laptop's lock and encryption state at the time of loss is unknown.

Under the lab's invented 1–3 rubric, the missing-device scenario scores 2 × 3 = 6, high. The visitor-entry scenario scores 1 × 2 = 2, low. Those are teaching judgements for the case date, not measured probabilities or an organization's acceptance threshold.

Publication boundary

This is a worked assessment of fictional records. It is not a real incident report, an independently assessed exercise, evidence of a confirmed data breach, or an approved exception. The packet authorizes reading and writing only; it authorizes no contact, device access or configuration change.

02 / Evidence boundary

Five facts. Several important gaps.

Each risk claim below traces to the supplied guided fixture. The packet contains no organization-wide risk tolerance, legal determination, approval record or technical control check.

04 / Control evidence

A plan is not an operating control.

01

Custody and reporting

G3 proposes locked storage, named checkout and prompt loss reporting. No checkout record, storage observation or reporting drill is supplied. A bounded authorized check would sample the custody record and walk through the approved reporting route.

02

Device and data protection

G2–G3 propose encryption, automatic lock and removing sensitive support fields from the offline cache. The loss-time state is unknown. An authorized test device and approved sample schema could check the proposed settings and data minimization; neither would prove the lost laptop's state.

03

Visitor access

G4 describes a closed badge door and a normal reception process; G5 identifies the rota at risk. No new entry-control check is supplied. The owner could review an authorized sample of the entry process before relying on it.

05 / Pending acceptance

A low score is not approval.

The fixture labels item-2 as accept but also records acceptance: false. The acceptance decision therefore remains pending. The person authorized to accept this risk, the organizational threshold, applicable obligations and a sign-off record are not supplied.

The fictional owner should revisit the request if entry is observed, the door is found open, the rota's importance changes, or the organization supplies a different tolerance. The historical 1 November review date cannot stand in for a present approval.

06 / Challenge and next checks

What would change the decision?

Alternative reading: if the laptop was encrypted and locked at loss time, the possible exposure could be narrower. G2 does not establish that state, so the report keeps the harm as a plausible scenario and asks for an authorized check. If the workroom door was open during visitor arrivals, entry opportunity would be stronger than G4's closed-door baseline; the published changed-case rehearsal tests that distinction without inventing an entry.

Decision request: the fictional owner should confirm the permitted reporting route, establish last known custody, seek authorized evidence of device protection and cache contents, assign control-check owners, identify the risk acceptance authority, and set a current review date. No real service-desk message or device action was taken for this case.

What remains open

The case provides no loss-time protection check, file-access evidence, approved acceptance, legal or policy determination, or independent reviewer assessment. The worksheet and reviewer guide show how to collect those distinctions in a later authorized exercise.
