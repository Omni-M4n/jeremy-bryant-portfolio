Governance, risk and compliance

Make a defensible decision from limited evidence.

Start with permission and scope. Identify what needs protection. Describe a risk, choose an owner and response, then show what the evidence supports.

These are fictional practice cases with offline checks. The checks validate defined rules and answer formats; they do not certify a control, establish legal compliance, or demonstrate independently assessed work.

Recommended GRC route

  1. Rules of engagement — define who may authorize work, the targets, allowed actions and stop conditions.
  2. Data classification — record purpose, handling, sharing and disposal decisions for fictional records.
  3. Vulnerability prioritisation — weigh evidence and business context before assigning urgency.
  4. Risk register — state the event and harm, owner, treatment, review date and residual uncertainty.
  5. Finding report — communicate the basis for a decision and a reproducible retest.

Evidence to save for each attempt

  • The exact case version, scope and source records used.
  • Your first decision and reasons, before opening the worked answer.
  • An alternative you considered, the owner or approver needed, and what remains unknown.
  • The check result, a changed-case attempt, and a reviewer note. Mark any step you did not perform.

Challenge assumptions against the source, identify missing evidence and propose a bounded validation step. Keep the first decision and any later correction distinct.

Framework context

The NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 describes high-level outcomes for governing and managing cybersecurity risk. NIST IR 8286A Rev. 1 discusses risk scenarios, likelihood, impact and risk registers. The scoring rules in these labs are teaching choices, not NIST scores or proof of compliance.
