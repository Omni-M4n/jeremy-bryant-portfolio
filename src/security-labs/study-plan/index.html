Start with what you can already show

Check your folders, tools and safe scope first. Bring saved work from a topic you know. Explain the facts, the help you used and what the result cannot prove. Then try a small case you have not seen. A reviewer can use that evidence to choose your next step.

Keep private coursework and personal data out of public examples. This plan does not ask you to install tools, create a VM or change cloud settings. Read each lab's scope before any real tool work.

Choose a pace you can keep

  • Gentle: four slots a week, each 20–25 minutes. The 12-week example starts with six packages and leaves 14 slots free.
  • Steady: eight slots a week. The 12-week example starts with twelve packages and leaves 32 slots free.

Both include four starting checks. Use free slots for longer tasks, another try, a review or rest. Split a long lab across days. The full plan has day tables; change them when your needs change.

Use five passes

  1. Work through a guided example. Pause to explain each step.
  2. Try on your own on another day. Save your answer before you check it.
  3. Solve a fresh case and explain it to a reviewer.
  4. About seven days after the fresh-case attempt, recall the method with different facts.
  5. About 28 days after the fresh-case attempt, try different facts again.

These are five passes, not a promise that five slots will be enough. Command help is allowed. Log all help. A check that says “pass” does not judge your reasons. Record a real tool attempt apart from work on supplied records.

Before a later topic, check the skills it needs. If a fresh case or reviewer is not ready, mark the attempt as awaiting review. Keep your first answer and corrections in a private journal.

Keep the plan and your evidence

The plan includes all topic stages, recall dates, a blank journal and safe ways to prepare for a beginner CTF or team event. It records no completed learner attempts.
