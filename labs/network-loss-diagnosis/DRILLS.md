# Practice sequence

You may use the README Answer vocabulary on every first attempt. It gives exact label spellings but does not match them to a case. Keep reference answers and `analyse` closed until you save your prediction.

1. **Guided:** prepare guided and draw the two endpoint columns. Write every byte range and your first explanation before opening the worked example.
2. **Prompts:** What does ACK 1100 say about bytes below 1100? Can a repeated range identify the cause of a retry? Can a zero capture-drop count prove complete coverage? What does the measured time interval include?
3. **Challenge:** prepare challenge in a new folder. Keep answers closed. Save each label with a frame reference. Make a table of possible causes, evidence and next checks. Mark evidence that is missing or conflicts with your view.
4. **Changed:** prepare changed in a new folder. Work out the time gap by hand. List at least two causes that the supplied evidence cannot tell apart. A long gap alone does not prove a fault in the program.
5. **Small edit:** follow the README steps for editing one input. Save a new `case-edited.json`. Shift one `data.packets` timestamp while keeping times in order. Predict the time gap, then compare the edited input. Keep `artifacts` empty. Keep the original case and first prediction.
6. **Teach-back:** use the missing delivery-photo example to explain why missing capture data differs from network loss. State where the comparison stops.
7. **Later recall:** after two days, write the byte range and ACK meaning without notes. Check your answer. Record corrections, help used and questions still open.

Any optional saved-capture review must follow the README's ownership limits. Keep that evidence separate. Handover includes first attempts and limits. A matching worksheet does not show that you used the real tool or assess your skill with it.
