Follow the [GRC path](https://jeremy-bryant.pages.dev/security-labs/grc/) or inspect its source exercises: [rules of engagement](https://github.com/Omni-M4n/jeremy-bryant-portfolio/blob/main/labs/rules-of-engagement/README.md), [data classification](https://github.com/Omni-M4n/jeremy-bryant-portfolio/blob/main/labs/data-classification/README.md), [vulnerability prioritisation](https://github.com/Omni-M4n/jeremy-bryant-portfolio/blob/main/labs/vulnerability-prioritisation/README.md), [risk register](https://github.com/Omni-M4n/jeremy-bryant-portfolio/blob/main/labs/risk-register/README.md) and [finding report](https://github.com/Omni-M4n/jeremy-bryant-portfolio/blob/main/labs/finding-report/README.md).

The [loan-cupboard case study](https://jeremy-bryant.pages.dev/security-labs/grc/loan-cupboard/) presents the worked decision and its evidence trail for portfolio readers.

## How to read the work

Most labs contain fictional cases and reference checks. They are learning materials, not a record of independent completion or a production assessment. The device case study records observations on owned hardware and states what remains unverified. A sound GRC review distinguishes source evidence, proposed response, control check, decision authority and remaining uncertainty; see the [GRC publication review](https://github.com/Omni-M4n/jeremy-bryant-portfolio/blob/main/docs/grc-publication-review.md).

For a specific lab, start with its `README.md`, make your own attempt, then read its worked example and run its checks. The [practice guide](https://github.com/Omni-M4n/jeremy-bryant-portfolio/blob/main/labs/PRACTICE-GUIDE.md) explains how to save evidence and revisit a changed case.
